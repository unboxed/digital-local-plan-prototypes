What is a local plan?

A local plan sets out how land can be used and developed over the next 15 to 20 years. It says how much new office space and how many homes are needed, where they should go, and which places should be protected.

It is written by the City of London Corporation, checked by an independent inspector, and shaped by what people tell us.

How does a local plan affect me?

The City Corporation uses the local plan to decide planning applications, from new towers to changes to shopfronts and homes.

It can affect what gets built near you, how tall new buildings can be, which open spaces are protected, and how streets are used.

What isn’t covered by the local plan?

The local plan doesn’t cover:

  • decisions on individual planning applications
  • building regulations and safety
  • things the government sets nationally, such as permitted development rights
  • day-to-day services like waste collection and street cleaning
