What is a local plan?

A local plan sets out how land in the city can be used and developed over the next 15 to 20 years. It says how many new homes and jobs we need, where they should go, and which places should be protected.

Every council in England must have one. It is written by the council, checked by an independent inspector, and shaped by what local people tell us.

How does a local plan affect me?

The council uses the local plan to decide planning applications, from large developments to changes to homes and businesses near you.

It can affect what gets built on your street, how tall new buildings can be, which green spaces are protected, and where new schools, shops and health centres go.

What isn’t covered by the local plan?

The local plan doesn’t cover:

  • decisions on individual planning applications
  • building regulations and safety
  • minerals and waste, which Hampshire’s minerals and waste plan covers
  • things the government sets nationally, such as permitted development rights
  • day-to-day services like bins, roads maintenance and parking permits

About this plan
